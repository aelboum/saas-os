# P Roadmap — Reusability

Status: three runs on 2026-10-11. **Run 3** (security remediation and the full final-tree validation) is recorded under "Run 3" below and supersedes run 2's validation table and its two open MEDIUM findings. Earlier text:  two runs on 2026-10-11. **Run 1** (authorized as P1–P8 with two reserved decisions) prepared the decisions and built the scaffold; its record is the per-phase "Outcome" entries below. **Run 2** (after the repository owner approved ADR-0038, ADR-0040, ADR-0041, ADR-0042 and confirmed ADR-0039) implemented them; its record is "Run 2" directly below, which supersedes any earlier "BLOCKED" or "awaiting decision" wording. Created from the eight gaps of the SaaS-OS reusability audit of 2026-10-11 (HEAD `4fccc93`, branch `tier2/tls-validation-infrastructure`). Nothing in either run is committed. It does not replace `docs/IMPLEMENTATION-ROADMAP.md`: Phases 0–9 there keep their own status, and every item that roadmap defers stays deferred (see "Deferred issue ledger").

The question this roadmap answers is narrow: **can an independent product, in its own repository, be built on SaaS-OS without modifying SaaS-OS?** No real product exists on top of the platform yet (ADR-0015 rule 12 keeps products out of this repository), so every phase below is validated against fixtures and clean environments, never against a product.

Every phase follows the template of `docs/IMPLEMENTATION-ROADMAP.md`: Objective, Files/Modules Affected, Dependencies, Tests, Security Requirements, Acceptance Criteria, Rollback Strategy, Outcome.

Status labels: **PASS** (all required checks ran and passed), **PASS WITH LIMITATIONS** (no known blocking defect; named checks unverified), **BLOCKED** (a decision, dependency or environment capability is missing), **FAIL** (an acceptance criterion is unmet).

## Inbound billing-provider events, gap G4 (2026-10-15)

Authorized by the repository owner, who approved ADR-0048 as drafted, with a 90-day ledger retention, and the refresh of this document's status tables.

- **What.** `build_stripe_billing_events_router()` serves `POST /v1/billing/providers/stripe/events`, a public route authenticated by Stripe's signature over the raw body. ## Stripe test-mode validation (2026-10-15)

Run against the owner's Stripe test-mode account (`sk_test_` key from the local `.env`, never printed), a disposable PostgreSQL 16 and Redis 7.

- **Defect found and fixed.** `StripeBillingProvider.create_subscription` failed against real Stripe: `This customer has no attached payment source or default payment method`. A subscription for a customer without a payment method was never creatable; every earlier test used an in-memory provider, and the live test was always skipped for want of a key. On the commercial path (which always passes a commerce context) the adapter now passes `payment_behavior=default_incomplete`: Stripe creates the subscription `incomplete` with an open first invoice, which the adapter already reads back as `pending` (ADR-0008 amendment: creation is not payment). The legacy `subscribe()` writes `active` at once, so it passes `payment_behavior=error_if_incomplete` and Stripe refuses any subscription whose first payment does not succeed: it fails closed; an independent review caught that a first version, which sent the parameter on both paths, let the legacy path grant entitlements for an unpaid subscription. Unit tests `test_stripe_creates_a_commercial_subscription_incomplete` and `test_stripe_legacy_subscription_is_not_created_incomplete`.
- **Live tests.** `tests/core/billing/test_billing_stripe_integration.py` now covers the commercial path: `pending` with no entitlements, the first invoice paid with Stripe's test card, `active` with entitlements after `reconcile_subscription_status`, `canceled` with none after `cancel_commercial_subscription`; and the legacy `subscribe()` refused for the missing payment method, with no row written. Negative controls: sending `default_incomplete` on the legacy path made the legacy test fail (`DID NOT RAISE`); sending nothing on the commercial path made the commercial test fail with Stripe's refusal. Its earlier legacy create-upgrade-cancel test never ran against Stripe (no payment method) and is replaced: a live plan change is not covered, since the commercial path has no plan change.
- **Real event deliveries.** A minimal app with only `build_stripe_billing_events_router()`, `stripe listen --forward-to` with the CLI's signing secret, and the commercial path (`create_subscription` with the real adapter, a platform-tenant merchant account for this Stripe account): created `pending` with no entitlements; the payer paid with the test card and an `invoice.paid` delivery made it `active` within 2 s with its entitlements; a cancellation in Stripe and its `customer.subscription.deleted` delivery made it `canceled` within 1 s with none. All four deliveries answered `200`, each recorded once (`processed`, `attempts=1`). A forged signature was `400`.
- **Failure paths, live (2026-10-15).** Three more tests in the same file, each on a Stripe test clock (the payer's customer is created on the clock by a test subclass of the adapter; deleting the clock removes the test's Stripe objects): a card declined on the first invoice (`card_declined`) leaves the subscription `pending` with no entitlements; a renewal charged to a declining card after a paid first month (the subscription's payment method, with a failed attempt on the open renewal invoice) makes it `past_due` with no entitlements; a first invoice left unpaid for two days expires at Stripe and reconciles to `canceled` with no entitlements, after which the payer can subscribe again (before, a second subscription is refused as `LiveSubscriptionExistsError`). Negative control: mapping Stripe's `past_due` to active and `incomplete_expired` to pending made the last two fail. All five live tests: 5 passed.
- **Payment page (ADR-0052, 2026-10-15).** The owner chose a SaaS-OS route that returns a payment URL ("option A"). `POST .../billing/subscriptions/{id}/payment-page` returns Stripe's hosted page for the open invoice of a `pending` or `past_due` commercial subscription, for a caller who may charge the payer; the card that pays becomes the subscription's own (`save_default_payment_method=on_subscription`). Live: page, payment, activation and a renewal charged to the same card on a test clock; and, by hand on 2026-10-16, a payment on the hosted page itself (with Stripe Link) that activated the subscription and became its default payment method. Evidence in the ADR.
- **Payment-page audit (ADR-0052 amendment, 2026-10-16).** The owner chose to audit issuance and refusals. A page handed out writes `billing.payment_page_issued` under the service tenant, in the transaction that makes the final authorization, status and active-parties check, plus `billing.payment_page_issued_for_account` under another payer. An authorization refusal before or after the provider call writes `billing.payment_page_refused` (`denied`). The URL and provider references are never recorded, and if the audit write fails, no URL is returned. Business refusals are not audited. Tests and six negative controls are in the ADR.
- **Open.** A commercial plan change does not exist; `change_plan` is reached only through the legacy `upgrade_subscription`, which refuses a commercial subscription. The adapter's `change_plan` itself is tested live since 2026-10-16 (`test_stripe_change_plan_moves_a_paid_subscription_to_the_new_price`: a paid subscription's only item moves to the new price, it stays `active`, a plan with no Stripe price is refused and changes nothing; negative control: an adapter that skips the price change fails the test). Stripe's default proration applies. Open, LOW, until a commercial plan change exists. A sponsorship migration of an inheriting tenant (`migrate_inherited_tenant_to_sponsorship`) for a payer without a payment method now completes with a `pending` subscription, so the tenant's inherited entitlements end until the first payment; before, Stripe refused the migration. Fail-closed for granting; make sure the payer can pay before migrating. Test-mode prices, customers and subscriptions created by the live tests are not archived. A replay of a Stripe idempotency key issued before this change (within Stripe's 24 hours) would be refused for differing parameters; earlier code could not create such subscriptions for customers without a payment method, and commercial recovery reuses recorded references. The same holds for the `payment_settings` parameter ADR-0052 adds.

### Administrator rule across the tenant hierarchy (B13 — closed)

Decision and design: ADR-0043, extended. The invariant is now: after every supported change, each tenant that had an administrator still has one. All eight scenarios demonstrated in run 5 are refused with `LastTenantAdministratorError`, which names the tenant that would be emptied:

| Scenario | Now |
|---|---|
| Parent: remove a SUBTREE role, revoke its permission, suspend the member, revoke a SUBTREE delegation, SUBTREE deny | Refused while it would empty a descendant; allowed once that descendant has an administrator of its own |
| `move_tenant` (away from the parent the authority came from, or under a parent that denies it) | Refused; the ancestry change is rolled back |
| Suspending or closing a parent tenant | Refused for the sake of a descendant; the tenant itself is exempt |
| A delegation of `membership_role:create` with an expiry | Never counted as the administrator a tenant may be left with, so the last non-expiring administrator cannot be removed |

How: the guard (`saas_os/core/rbac/administrators.py`) judges the mutation's tenant and every descendant in which an affected user is an active member; `move_tenant` and `transition_tenant_status` judge the moved subtree, respectively the descendants. Per-tenant advisory locks are taken ancestor first, before the tenant-row locks. `can()` gained one optional argument, `ignore_expiring_delegations` (default `False`, passed only by the guard). No migration, no role hierarchy, no change to any authorization decision.

Behaviour change to note: a parent tenant, and an operator moving or suspending a tenant, can now be refused. The remedy is in the error — give the named tenant an administrator of its own first.

Evidence: `tests/core/rbac/test_last_administrator_hierarchy_integration.py`, 47 tests, with the two earlier files 104 in total; five cross-tenant races, 28 runs, each ending with an administrator.

**Found by CI, fixed:** the first CI run of these tests hit a deadlock once, in the race between a parent's deny and a child's deny. The invariant held (PostgreSQL failed one transaction) but a caller would have received a database error. Cause: `create_deny` locked the denied user's row before the administrator locks of the tenants it reaches. The order was reversed; a test now forces that interleaving and fails with the old order. Two narrower deadlocks of the same kind remain possible and are described in ADR-0043; they abort one transaction and change nothing. Negative controls: with the check removed, a parent mutation, a move and a suspension each empty the child; with the descendant search off, a parent mutation empties the child. **One control did not fail:** with the descendant locks removed by hand, the cross-tenant races still pass (44 runs) — the row locks `can()` takes already serialize them, so no administrator is lost without those locks. What their order decides is waiting versus deadlocking, which the forced-interleaving test demonstrates.

Not covered (ADR-0043): user deactivation (no Core function), purge and erasure, and a tenant attached beneath a tenant in the instant it is suspended.

## Consumer-readiness assessment

**Can an independent product be built on SaaS OS without modifying SaaS OS?** Yes for the backend. The work is published: `main` at `58d134f` (run 7), with ADR-0045's platform authority at `3677c86` (2026-10-14), with the purge caller and the operator login rule at `8b5e458` (2026-10-14), with invitation acceptance over HTTP at `9fd35b3` (2026-10-14), with API-key request authentication at `6a71d70` (2026-10-15), with billing-provider events and the production hardening at `93b8002` (2026-10-15), with the Stripe test-mode fix at `26ec1de` (2026-10-15), and with the payment page at `4197d2f` (2026-10-16), which both example pins name.

Proven on the final working tree, from an installed wheel outside the repository and on the full integration gate: one unambiguous import root; installation with or without each provider SDK; both migration histories; a product schema with forced RLS; a permission-gated product route; a product job on the platform worker; tenant purge of product data; platform routers for membership, invitations, a member's own API keys, billing subscriptions and usage, each refused without its permission and to other tenants; a tenant that cannot lose its last administrator through membership changes; and cookie-authenticated mutations that cannot be forged from another origin.

What a first product still has to do or accept:

1. **Pin `4197d2f` or later.** Earlier commits have no payment page, and from `26ec1de` they create Stripe subscriptions whose renewals cannot use the card that paid (ADR-0052); before `26ec1de` they cannot create a Stripe subscription for a payer without a payment method; before `93b8002` they lack billing-provider events, the failed-credential limit, operator multi-factor login and the operator commands; before `6a71d70` they lack API-key request authentication and the platform mutation timeout; before `9fd35b3` they lack the invitation acceptance router; before `8b5e458` they lack `purge_tenant`'s required caller and the operator login rule; before `3677c86` they lack ADR-0045's required callers and platform authority; before `58d134f` they also lack the `saas_os` import root, the worker seam and the extras.
2. **Send `Origin` on cookie-authenticated mutations** — a browser does; a script must, or use a Bearer header.
3. **Give `membership_role:create` to more than one person per tenant.** Since run 6 a change that would leave a tenant without an administrator is refused, including from a parent tenant; an expiring delegation does not count (ADR-0043).
4. **Write its own**: sign-up and tenant creation, the invitation link and email that lead to `invitation_acceptance_router`, the choice of which of its own routes accept an API key (`require_permission(..., api_key=True)`), webhook and feature-flag administration, usage ingestion, and its whole frontend. Receiving billing-provider events is an opt-in router since ADR-0048 (2026-10-15); a product that mounts it configures `STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET` and installs the retention timer.

Unproven: any real product; live Stripe, ZITADEL and GitHub paths. Remote CI (Linux) passed on the merged head; the local runs were on Windows.
