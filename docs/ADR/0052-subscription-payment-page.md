# ADR-0052: Payment page for a subscription

Status: Accepted (2026-10-15) and implemented — the repository owner chose a SaaS-OS route that returns a payment URL, with the existing provider events activating the subscription ("option A").
Date: 2026-10-15
Supersedes / Superseded by: — (schedules the payment step that ADR-0029 deferred as "checkout sessions")
Related: ADR-0008 (amendment points 1, 2, 8), ADR-0029 (payer, merchant, authority), ADR-0030 (route policy), ADR-0031 (callers; the provider-call boundary), ADR-0040 (opt-in capability routers), ADR-0048 (provider events).

## Context

- A commercial subscription is created by Core first: `create_subscription()` asks the adapter to create the provider subscription, then writes the row `pending`. Since 2026-10-15 the Stripe adapter creates it `incomplete` with an open first invoice (`payment_behavior=default_incomplete`), because Stripe refuses a payer without a payment method otherwise.
- Nothing in SaaS OS lets the payer pay that invoice. A product had to call Stripe itself with the provider reference, which the HTTP API deliberately does not return.
- When the payer pays, Stripe's events (ADR-0048) or `reconcile_subscription_status` move the row to `active`. A failed renewal moves it to `past_due`, with an open invoice of its own.
- Paying the first invoice does not, by default, make the card the subscription's payment method: the renewal would then find none and fail.

Two Stripe mechanisms collect a payment through a Stripe-hosted page:

1. **Checkout Sessions** (`mode=subscription`). Checkout creates the subscription itself when the payer completes it. That inverts Core's order: the provider subscription would exist before, and independently of, the authorized, idempotent, audited `create_subscription()`, and its reference would have to be adopted from an event. It also leaves the subscription and its invoice in a state SaaS OS did not create.
2. **The hosted invoice page** (`hosted_invoice_url` of the open invoice). It pays an invoice of a subscription that already exists. Stripe hosts it, accepts the payment methods the account enables, and handles authentication (3-D Secure).

## Decision

1. **A payment page is the provider's hosted page for the subscription's open invoice.** For Stripe: the subscription's latest invoice, when it is `open`, and its `hosted_invoice_url`. Checkout Sessions are not used (Context, 1): Core keeps creating the subscription, so its authorization, idempotency, one-live-subscription rule and audit stay where they are.
2. **A separate, optional provider capability.** `PaymentPageProvider` (a runtime-checkable protocol) with `payment_page_url(*, provider_subscription_id) -> str | None`: the URL, or `None` when nothing is due. It is not added to `BillingProvider`, so an existing adapter written by a product stays valid; Core refuses an adapter without it (`PaymentPageNotSupportedError`). No provider type crosses the interface (ADR-0008 point 8): a URL string or nothing. The Stripe adapter and `FakeBillingProvider` implement it.
3. **Core: `get_payment_page(caller, *, service_tenant_id, subscription_id, provider=None) -> str`.**
   - Authority: `billing.account:charge` at the subscription's payer, the authority that `create_subscription()` already requires for the payer (ADR-0029): the page charges the payer. Checked in the transaction that reads the row; refused with `BillingNotAuthorizedError`.
   - The payer checked is the one recorded on the subscription row, never one taken from the request; when the payer is another tenant, `billing.account:charge` at the service tenant is not enough.
   - Only for a verified commercial subscription that is `pending` or `past_due`; any other status, or a legacy row, is refused (`NoPaymentDueError`, `LegacyReconciliationError`). The billing account and the merchant account must be active, as for creating a subscription (`CommercialPartyNotActiveError`).
   - The provider reference is re-verified against SaaS OS's own index before the provider is asked, as reconciliation does, and the adapter is the merchant's (`_adapter_for_merchant`), so a page is never fetched from another account.
   - The provider is called outside any database transaction (ADR-0031). After the call the authorization, the status gate and the active-parties check are repeated before the URL is returned: authority withdrawn, the subscription paid or canceled, or a party suspended meanwhile means no URL. The URL is returned, never stored. No status changes here: activation stays with provider events and reconciliation.
   - No audit row: nothing changes, as for the other billing reads.
4. **HTTP: `POST /v1/tenants/{tenant_id}/billing/subscriptions/{subscription_id}/payment-page`** in `build_billing_router()`, returning `{"url": ...}`. The path's tenant is the service tenant, as for cancel; the route policy is `billing.subscription:read` there, and Core checks the payer's `billing.account:charge`. `404` for an unknown subscription, `403` without authority, `409` ("Nothing is due for this subscription.") when nothing is payable, `409` when the subscription or its adapter has no payment page, `503` with `Retry-After` when the provider cannot be asked. `POST`, not `GET`: it asks the provider, and the URL is not to be cached or prefetched; the same-origin check (ADR-0044) applies to cookie callers.
5. **The card that pays the first invoice pays the renewals.** On the commercial path the Stripe adapter also sends `payment_settings.save_default_payment_method=on_subscription`, so Stripe makes the payment method that paid an invoice the subscription's default.
6. **Not in scope.** Trials, coupons, tax collection settings, a return URL after payment (the hosted invoice page has none; the product shows its own page and the subscription's status), payment-method management for an active subscription, and Stripe Connect.

## Consequences

- A product mounts nothing new: the billing router it already mounts offers the payment page. Its frontend sends the payer to the URL and polls the subscription (or the entitlements) for `active`.
- The page belongs to Stripe's invoice: it expires with the invoice (an unpaid first invoice expires after about a day, and the subscription becomes `canceled`). A product asks for a new subscription then.
- A product with its own `BillingProvider` gets `409` from the route until its adapter implements `PaymentPageProvider`.
- The same route pays a failed renewal (`past_due`): the open renewal invoice is the page. Only the subscription's *latest* invoice is considered: if Stripe has already issued a newer invoice that is not open (a draft, or a paid zero-amount invoice), the route answers `409` while the row is still `past_due`, and an older open invoice is paid through Stripe's own reminders or dashboard. This fails closed.
- A subscription that no longer exists at Stripe owes nothing here (`409`), not a provider outage (`503`).
- The route is under the service tenant's path, so the caller needs `billing.subscription:read` there as well as `billing.account:charge` at the payer. A payer with authority only in its own tenant (a sponsor with no role in the sponsored tenant) cannot ask for the page; someone with both can, and passes the URL on.
- **Subscriptions created before this ADR** (commits from `26ec1de` up to the merge of this ADR) were created without `save_default_payment_method` (Stripe records `off`): paying their first invoice does not make the card theirs, so each renewal fails to `past_due` and has to be paid on the page. This fails closed: entitlements end, none are granted.
  - *Deployment status (2026-10-16).* The repository has no release, tag, GitHub deployment or environment, and the owner reports no deployment of those commits. The repository's Stripe key is a test-mode key; a production Stripe account was not read, so the absence of affected subscriptions there is not proven. The example pins named `26ec1de` from 2026-10-15 until they moved to `4197d2f` (this ADR's merge) on 2026-10-16, so a product that copied a pin in that window is exposed.
  - *Recovery (operator-run, per subscription, in the merchant's own Stripe account; SaaS OS ships no bulk change).* Find the Stripe subscriptions whose `payment_settings.save_default_payment_method` is `off` and whose ID is the `provider_subscription_id` of a `pending`, `active` or `past_due` row in `core.billing_subscriptions`. For each, set `payment_settings.save_default_payment_method=on_subscription`; that covers the next invoice paid. For one already `active` without a `default_payment_method`, also set `default_payment_method` to the payment method that paid its first invoice, after checking that the method is attached to that subscription's own customer. Neither change touches SaaS OS's rows or entitlements; the next provider event or reconciliation does.
- The URL is a bearer link to pay the invoice. It is returned only to a caller who may charge the payer, is not stored, and is not logged by SaaS OS. Whoever pays through it makes their payment method the subscription's default for renewals (decision 5). Issuing it writes no audit row (decision 3); auditing it would be a separate decision.

## Validation (2026-10-15)

- **Live, Stripe test mode** (`tests/core/billing/test_billing_stripe_integration.py::test_stripe_payment_page_then_renewal_on_the_same_card`, on a Stripe test clock): a new commercial subscription's page is Stripe's hosted page for its open first invoice; paying that invoice with the test card activates it (entitlements granted); then nothing is due (`NoPaymentDueError`); the card is the subscription's default payment method; a month later the renewal invoice is paid with it and the subscription stays `active`. Negative control: without `save_default_payment_method=on_subscription` the subscription had no default payment method after the first payment, and the test failed.
- **HTTP** (`tests/api/v1/test_capability_routers_integration.py::test_payment_page_is_for_the_payer_while_something_is_due`, fake provider): `409` while active and after cancellation; `200` with the provider's URL while `past_due`; `403` for a member with `billing.subscription:read` but no `billing.account:charge` at the payer, `200` with both; `404` through another tenant's path and for an unknown subscription; `503` with `Retry-After` when the provider cannot be asked. The route is in the route-policy table and the authorization matrix. Negative control: checking the payer for `billing.subscription:read` instead of `billing.account:charge` let the reader through, and the test failed.
- **Core** (`test_payment_page_core_refusals`): each protection refuses on its own -- the status gate with an adapter that would always offer a page, an adapter without the capability, a provider reference that no longer resolves to the row, a suspended merchant account, and, during the provider call, a merchant suspended, the subscription canceled, or authority withdrawn (no URL returned in each case).
- **The row's payer** (`test_payment_page_needs_charge_at_the_rows_own_payer`): with another tenant as payer, `billing.account:charge` at the service tenant gets `403`; adding it at the payer gets the page.
- **What the live test does not show:** it pays the invoice through the API with a test card attached to the customer beforehand, as the page would charge it; a card typed into the hosted page itself was not exercised. Stripe documents `save_default_payment_method=on_subscription` for any payment that pays the subscription's invoice.
- **Same-origin:** the route is in the forged-cookie table of `tests/api/test_csrf_unit.py`.
- **Unit:** the Stripe adapter's exact parameters on both paths, and its page: only the open latest invoice yields a URL; a paid, draft, void, missing or unexpanded invoice yields none; an open invoice without a hosted page and any Stripe error other than a missing subscription raise `BillingProviderError`.
